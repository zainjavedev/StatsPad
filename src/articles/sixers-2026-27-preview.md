Philadelphia opens 2026–27 with the most talented starting five it has had in years. Tyrese Maxey, VJ Edgecombe, Jaylen Brown, LeBron James and Joel Embiid combined for **120.8 points per game** last season. Here is what the 2025–26 numbers say about how it fits together.

## Two of the league's top five scorers

Jaylen Brown (28.7 points a game, **4th in the NBA**) and Tyrese Maxey (28.3, **5th**) now share a backcourt and wing. Add Embiid (26.9, 10th) and LeBron (20.9, 34th) and the Sixers start four of last season's top 35 scorers.

The question is less whether they can score than who gives up the ball. Maxey led the league in minutes at **38.0 a game**, and both he and Brown were used as first options last season.

## The shooting is fine, not great

None of the five starters made more than 37% of their threes last season:

- Maxey 36.7% on 8.6 attempts a game
- Edgecombe 35.4%
- Brown 34.7%
- Embiid 33.3%
- LeBron 31.7%

That is why the bench matters. **Anfernee Simons** shot 38.5% on 6.9 threes a game, the best mark on the roster among regular shooters, and **Kentavious Caldwell-Pope** made 91.3% of his free throws. Justin Edwards (37.2%) and Dean Wade (36.2%) give the second and third units spacing too.

## Health is the swing factor

Embiid played **38 games** last season and LeBron turns 42 in December. Edgecombe (75 games) and Maxey (70) carried the load, and Brown played 71 for Boston. If Embiid gets near 60 games, this lineup's ceiling is as high as anyone's in the East.

## The bench to watch

- **Dean Wade** is the glue forward, 4.2 rebounds in 22 minutes with Cleveland.
- **Dominick Barlow** played 71 games last season and gives the second unit size.
- **Adem Bona** starts camp working back from a foot injury.
- **Labaron Philon Jr.** is the rookie guard with no NBA minutes yet.
